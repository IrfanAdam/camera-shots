01 — Vision

Cinema is a language, not a lottery.

Every great shot answers a question before it fills a frame: whose eyes are we in, how close do we stand, what moves and what holds still. The product treats those answers as the work itself.

The engine is the product: scenes, shots, locations, props, and cast composed into reusable prompt tokens. The wiki sits alongside as a learning reference — size, framing, angle, movement, light, composition the way directors actually use it — so you never have to guess what a token means. The thesis is simple — deliberate intent in, deliberate cinema out.

Direct with intent: every shot has a job before it has a look.

— the working rule behind the engine

02 — Problem

Prompt roulette is not directing.

Type a paragraph, get four random clips, repeat until something looks right. That is the current workflow — and it fails exactly where cinema matters most.

Prompt roulette

Long prose prompts with no shot grammar. Every run is a fresh gamble; nothing learned carries forward.

Style drift

Shot three forgets shot one. Focal length, light, and palette wander because nothing pins them down.

Weak shot language

Vague verbs — "cinematic, dramatic" — instead of named decisions: close-up or wide, dolly or lock, key from the side or golden backlight.

No continuity

Screen direction flips, eyelines break, coverage never gets planned. The edit inherits the mess.

03 — The Craft Gap

Between taste and tools, your craft is left in the lurch.

Filmmakers know what good looks like. Generators make pixels fast. What is missing is the craft layer: naming the shot, justifying it, and holding it across a sequence.

  • Knowledge is scattered. Shot craft lives across textbooks, essays, and long video series — rarely in one working reference.
  • Planning has no home. Shot lists live in docs, stills in folders, tokens in chat logs. Nothing connects the beat to the phrase that describes it.
  • Prompts are not reusable. Good phrasing gets typed once and lost. Tokens like CU — close-up, face fills the frame should be copy-ready, every time.

04 — How It Works

Board it. Pin it. Export it.

Three moves inside the engine — no accounts, no rendering, no model lock-in. The wiki backs you up when a term needs explaining. The output is deliberate language you paste wherever you generate.

1. Board the sequence — the engine

Storyboard scenes and shots; track locations, props, and cast. Structure first, pixels later.

2. Pin continuity, compose tokens

Every card composes a prompt preview with its anchor first — lens, light, palette, and screen direction held across the sequence. Stuck on a term? The wiki has the what, when, and token (CU, DOLLY, GOLD) ready to copy.

3. Export deliberate intent

Copy a tokenised prompt — SHOT-XX — phrase — and paste it into any generator, brief, or shot list. The craft travels; the tool stays out of the way.

05 — Built For

People who care about the frame — and the story it tells.

Not for chasing trends — for anyone who wants AI footage to land like a story, not an accident: decided frames, deliberate beats.

Directors

Cinematographers

AI filmmakers

Storytellers

Shape beats that cut together — intent, coverage, and continuity decided on the board before a pixel renders.

Students of craft

Learn why a close-up lands, when a dolly beats a zoom, and how screen direction holds.

06 — Principles

What the work refuses to do.

  • Intent before pixels. Name the job of the shot before choosing its look.
  • Tokens over prose. Short, named, reusable phrases beat paragraphs that vanish.
  • Structure over generation. A storyboarded sequence with anchors beats four lucky clips. The product generates nothing — on purpose.
  • Craft is portable. What you learn and compose here works in any generator, brief, or edit.

07 — What's Next

Deepen what already exists.

No new promises — the near-term direction is making the engine loop sharper: deeper composing first, with the wiki kept consistent as backup.

  • Now — more storyboard and composer depth in the engine. Richer shot and sequence composing, so anchors and continuity hold across scenes.
  • Next — keep the wiki sharp as backup. Entries, when-to-use guidance, and copy-ready tokens stay consistent, so lookup never breaks flow.
  • Later — tighten the engine → intent loop. Make composing a board and exporting the prompt feel like one motion, so craft carries straight into the generator.

Roadmap

Gates, not dates.

Work runs in series where one output feeds the next, and in parallel where tracks are independent. A gate opens the next work only when its exit criteria are met. Windows are ceilings, not promises.

Tracks

  • Engine (E): storyboard, composer, anchors, continuity — the product itself
  • Wiki (W): shot entries and copy-ready tokens — a learning reference and backup, parallel to E
  • Loop (P): composing a board and exporting its tokenised prompt as one motion

Dependency map

  • Serial: G0 → G1 → G2. Each gate depends on the one before.
  • Parallel: W runs alongside E from G0 and never blocks a gate.
  • Join: P begins at G1, once continuity holds across a sequence.

Gates

G0 · Composer holds (≤ 8 wks)

Entry: none. Exit: 3-scene board with locked lens, light, palette, and screen direction; 5 filmmakers compose a board unassisted. Opens: composer depth (E); wiki consistency (W), in parallel.

G1 · Continuity at length (≤ 10 wks)

Entry: G0. Exit: 10-shot sequence exports with no reviewer-flagged continuity breaks; ≥90% of tokens used are copy-ready; wiki covers every token used. Opens: Loop (P).

G2 · One motion (≤ 10 wks)

Entry: G1. Exit: compose-to-export runs as one flow; 20 weekly-active makers; ≥40% of active users say they would be "very disappointed" without the product (Sean Ellis test). Opens: decision on B2C scale spend.

Kill and pivot rules

  • Gate fails once: close the criteria gap and re-run the gate.
  • Gate fails twice: revisit the upstream assumption (composer or continuity), not downstream work.
  • G3 misses conversion: hold growth spend and re-test price before adding features.

Purpose

The operating layer for the visual twin.

Camera Shots is the enabling layer that gives an organisation or a person a shared, structured source of truth for the visual part of their digital twin. It does not replace the twin. It supplies the shared visual language the twin is built from, so that the story an org or a life tells about itself stays coherent, traceable, and owned by the people it belongs to.

An organisation's story is scattered across decks, docs, chats, footage, and memory. Camera Shots brings the visual part of that story into one shared context: what has been shown, why it was shown, how it should look, and what has been kept or retired.

Principles

Four rules the build obeys.

Intent before pixels.

Why: every visual element must declare its job in the story before its look, or the story has nothing to hold together.

Shared truth over private drafts.

Why: the twin is only useful if the people who tell the story work from the same source, not from whichever copy someone saved last.

Owned narrative.

Why: the team decides what the story says and what gets left for posterity. The enabler records those decisions; it does not make them.

Craft is portable.

Why: plain-text tokenised entries work in any tool, brief, or edit. The story is never locked to one platform or model.

Architecture

Four layers, from shared context to export.

01 — Context

The shared source of truth: the org's or person's visual record, with each entry tagged by purpose, period, and owner. This is what the twin is built from.

02 — Tokens

Every visual entry emits a named, versioned token (for example CU, DOLLY, GOLD). Tokens are the shared vocabulary, so the same idea means the same thing across every part of the story.

03 — Compose and continuity

Scenes are assembled from entries. Anchors (lens, light, palette, screen direction, tone) are pinned first and held across the sequence, so the visual story stays consistent as it grows.

04 — Export

Model-agnostic, plain-text tokenised output that any team member, tool, or generator can use. This is how the visual twin reaches decks, briefs, shot lists, and archives.

Narrative and posterity

Who owns the story, and what is kept.

  • The team owns the narrative. Camera Shots records and structures it. It does not decide it.
  • Decisions are explicit. Each entry can be kept, revised, or retired, with a reason, so the record shows how the story changed.
  • Posterity is a choice. What gets left for the future is curated on purpose, not left to whatever happened to be saved.
  • A person's story works the same way. The same layer can hold a single life's visual record, owned by that person.

Use cases

Where the enabler applies.

Organisations

A shared visual source of truth for how the company tells its story internally and externally.

Individuals

A personal visual record, owned and curated by the person whose life it covers.

Applications built on the layer

Any product that needs a consistent visual context can draw from the same tokens and anchors.

  • Local-first data. Records live in the user's browser today, so there is no server-side store to breach.
  • No accounts, no secrets yet. No passwords, tokens, or payment data exist in the system today.

Scope

In, out, and when out comes back.

In scope

Wiki entries linked to prompt fields · versioned token library · scene→shot locked templates · continuity ledger · one-action-per-shot checker · model-agnostic export.

Out of scope

No model training — re-entry: never, product generates nothing. No rendering pipeline — re-entry: only via tokenised prompt export. No accounts/billing — re-entry: when teams need sync.

Requirements

FR-01 to FR-06, each with a done-when gate.

FR-01 — Wiki-linked prompt fields

Every wiki entry maps to generatable prompt fields.

Done when: each entry renders its fields + copy token.

FR-02 — Versioned token library

Tokens carry versions; phrasing improves without breaking boards.

Done when: token edit creates a version; old boards pin the old one.

FR-03 — Scene→shot locked templates

Beats break into shots with anchors pinned at creation.

Done when: new shot inherits scene anchors, locked by default.

FR-04 — Continuity ledger

Lens, light, palette, screen direction held across the sequence.

Done when: changing a ledger value flags every affected shot.

FR-05 — One-action-per-shot checker

Flags shots trying to do two jobs at once.

Done when: two-verb shots warn with a one-click split.

FR-06 — Model-agnostic export

Plain-text tokenised prompts paste into any generator.

Done when: board exports to clipboard as SHOT-XX tokenised prompts with zero lab-specific syntax.

Arch principles

How the build stays small.

  • Phase-gated: each backlog phase starts only when the prior gate passes.
  • Single-adapter first: one ingest (wiki terms) → one timeline (shot board) → one artifact (tokenised prompt).
  • Scenario, not forecast: all money math is labelled scenario-model, never commitment.
  • Static until sync: no backend until teams demand cross-device boards.
